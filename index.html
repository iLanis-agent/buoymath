Buoy Math

A 12 kg body in 20 L has a density of 600 kg/m³. In a 1000 kg/m³ fluid it sits 60% under the surface and can take 8 kg more before it is awash.

Boundary: arithmetic only Buoy Math works out density, displacement and spare lift from the numbers you type. The mass, volume and fluid density are yours to measure or read off your own setup. It treats every body as uniform and every fluid as still, so it has not seen your setup and does not know the body's shape, balance, leaks or condition, the water's movement, or how anything is loaded or rigged, and it gives no verdict that anything is safe to float, ride, tow or load. Every number is your own entry.

Starting examples: a 12 kg body of 20 L in a 1000 kg/m³ fluid, how much of it sits under, and how much more it can take before it is awash.

The density truth

A 12 kg body in 20 L has a density of 600 kg/m³. The fluid is 1000 kg/m³, so the body is less dense than the fluid.

The draft truth

A 12 kg body in 20 L, in a 1000 kg/m³ fluid: 60% of its volume sits under the surface and 40% above it.

The lift truth

Fully under, the 1000 kg/m³ fluid buoys 20 kg against the 12 kg body. That leaves 8 kg of spare lift before it sits awash.
